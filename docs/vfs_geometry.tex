\emph{Proposition} below is a closed-form result; every \emph{Corollary} is an interpretive reading

are kept explicit throughout:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]

only on a measure-zero locus (\S5, \S7).

\section{The Natural Metric on the Live Domain}

\subsection*{The barrier is a geodesic distance}
The core barrier $B(r)=-\ln(1-r)$ is the arc length of a one-dimensional conformal metric on
$r\in[0,1)$:
\[
\boxed{ds_{\rm rad}=\frac{dr}{1-r},\qquad
\int_0^r\frac{d\rho}{1-\rho}=-\ln(1-r)=B(r).}
\]
\note{Thus the radial Lyapunov term $A_rB(r)$ is, up to scale, the geodesic distance from the
synergy-free state $r=0$; the death wall $r\to1^-$ sits at infinite metric distance.}

\subsection*{The capacity-scaled metric and the imbalance direction}
Writing $u=r\Op$ and sending the wall to infinity gives the radial part $du^2/\mu^2$ with
$\mu=1-u/\Op$. The imbalance direction is warped by the alignment factor, because the alignment
force in $\dot\Delta=-(\mu a+\rho)\Delta$ is exactly $\mu a$, linear in $\mu$, and vanishes at the
wall. The natural live-domain metric is therefore the warped product
\[
\boxed{ds^2=\frac{du^2}{\mu^2}+\mu^{2p}\,d\Delta^2,
\qquad \mu=1-\frac{u}{\Op},\qquad p>0.}
\]
\begin{definition}[Canonical warp]
The canonical exponent is $p=1$, i.e.\ $\mu^{2}\,d\Delta^2$, matching the linear $\mu a$ dependence
of the alignment force. The qualitative results below hold for every $p\neq0$; only the curvature
radius depends on $p$.
\end{definition}

\section{The Live Domain is Hyperbolic}

\subsection*{Naive reading is flat}
With an \emph{unwarped} imbalance direction ($p=0$, $g_{\Delta\Delta}=1$) the metric
$ds^2=du^2/\mu^2+d\Delta^2$ is flat:
\[
\boxed{R\equiv0\qquad(p=0),}
\]
since $\tilde u=-\Op\ln\mu=\Op B$ straightens it into Euclidean $d\tilde u^2+d\Delta^2$.
\note{Under the minimal reading the core supplies, there is no hidden geometry: the dynamics is a
forced flow on a flat state space. Curvature can only come from the dynamically required warp.}

\subsection*{Constant negative curvature}
\begin{proposition}[Hyperbolicity of the live domain]\label{prop:hyp}
For the warped metric $ds^2=du^2/\mu^2+\mu^{2p}d\Delta^2$ with $\mu=1-u/\Op$, the scalar curvature is
constant and strictly negative:
\[
\boxed{\;R=-\frac{2p^2}{\Op^2}=\mathrm{const}<0,\qquad
\Ric=-\frac{p^2}{\Op^2}\,g.\;}
\]
In the canonical case $p=1$,
\[
\boxed{R=-\frac{2}{\Op^2},\qquad K=-\frac{1}{\Op^2},\qquad
\text{curvature radius }=\Op.}
\]
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} In arc length $s=-\Op\ln\mu$ the warp is $w=\mu^p=e^{-ps/\Op}$, so the
$2$D warped product $ds^2=ds^2_{\rm rad}+w^2d\Delta^2$ has Gaussian curvature
$K=-w''/w=-(p/\Op)^2$, and $R=2K$. Verified directly in $(u,\Delta)$ coordinates. \hfill$\square$

\note{The live domain is a piece of the hyperbolic plane $\mathbb H^2$ whose curvature radius is the
Brim $\Op$ itself.}

\section{The Brim Flow as a Forced Conformal Flow}

\begin{proposition}[Brim conformal flow]\label{prop:flow}
Under the Brim law $\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda$ the capacity-scaled metric evolves conformally,
\[
\boxed{\partial_t g=2\,\frac{\dot\Op}{\Op}\,g=2\,\frac{\alpha\lambda(t)}{\Op}\,g
=2\psi(t)\,g,\qquad \psi=\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\Op}\ge0.}
\]
\end{proposition}

\subsection*{Comparison with Ricci flow}
On the hyperbolic surface of Prop.~\ref{prop:hyp} ($p=1$), $\Ric=-\Op^{-2}g$, so Ricci flow would read
\[
\boxed{\partial_t g=-2\Ric=+\frac{2}{\Op^2}\,g.}
\]
Both flows are conformal \emph{expansions} of a negatively curved metric (same sign, same type). They
coincide exactly iff the forcing rate equals the curvature rate:
\[
\boxed{\frac{2\alpha\lambda}{\Op}=\frac{2}{\Op^2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha\,\lambda\,\Op=1.}
\]
\begin{remark}
The actual V.F.S.\ flow is \emph{forced}: its rate is set by the external Sophia field $\lambda$, not
by the surface's own curvature. It is ``Ricci-flow-like'' in type, with the forcing $\alpha\lambda$
standing in for the curvature as the rate. The self-driven (genuinely intrinsic) regime is the
locus $\alpha\lambda\Op=1$ studied in \S\ref{sec:locus}.
\end{remark}

\section{Epektasis as Curvature Relaxation}
Along a continuous live arc the curvature drifts monotonically toward zero:
\[
\boxed{\dot R=\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(-\frac{2}{\Op^2}\right)=\frac{4\dot\Op}{\Op^3}
=\frac{4\alpha\lambda}{\Op^3}\ \ge0\quad(\lambda\ge0).}
\]
Hence, with sustained Sophia, $\Op\to\infty$ and
\[
\boxed{|R|=\frac{2}{\Op^2}\ \xrightarrow[\;\Op\to\infty\;]{}\ 0.}
\]
\note{Epektasis is the relaxation of hyperbolic curvature to flatness: the state space asymptotically
flattens as Sophia expands the Brim. Theosis as a global flattening that no finite stage attains.}
\note{This concerns the \emph{spatial} live-surface only. In the cosmological lift the full spacetime
curvature also depends on $\ddot\Op$ (equivalently $\dot\lambda$), not on $\Op$ alone --- see
\S\ref{sec:cosmo-bridge}.}

\section{Resurrectio: a Curvature Discontinuity}\label{sec:reset}
The hybrid reset uses $\Op^+=\Op^-+\kappa_R\Grec^-$ (theological corollary 12). Since $R=-2/\Op^2$
depends \emph{only} on $\Op$, the curvature jumps even though $\sigma,\lambda,u$ never enter it:

The reset decomposes geometrically into a \emph{motion of the point} (the jump in $V,F$, hence in

Let $\xi:=\alpha\lambda\Op$ measure the excess of forcing over intrinsic curvature rate; $\xi=1$ is the

On $\xi=1$, $\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda=1/\Op$, whence

\note{This is exactly the Ricci-flow rate for a hyperbolic surface ($|R|\sim1/t$). Sitting on the

With sustained $\lambda\to\lambda_\infty>0$ the Brim grows \emph{linearly},

\section{Additional Geometric Developments}\label{sec:additions}

The preceding sections establish the main geometric core: the live-surface is hyperbolic,
the Brim is its curvature radius, and Sophia drives a forced conformal expansion. This section
adds five refinements that became visible after separating the geometric layer from the
cosmological layer.

\subsection{Two Thresholds: Ricci Resonance and Zero-Density}

The Brim law appears in both Geometry and Cosmology, but the two layers read different
thresholds from it. In the geometric layer the key comparison is between the Sophia-driven
Brim-flow and the intrinsic Ricci-rate of the live-surface. Since
\[
\partial_t g_\Sigma=2\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\Op}g_\Sigma,
\qquad
-2\Ric_\Sigma=\frac{2}{\Op^2}g_\Sigma,
\]
the two rates coincide precisely when
\[
\boxed{\xi:=\alpha\lambda\Op=1.}
\]
This is the \emph{Ricci-resonance threshold}. It answers the geometric question:
\[
\text{Does Sophia-driven Brim-flow match the curvature's own Ricci-like rate?}
\]

The cosmological layer asks a different question. In the Lorentzian lift
\[
ds^2_{\rm Vessel}=-dt^2+\Op(t)^2h_{2D},
\]
the reconstructed effective density is
\[
\rho_{\rm eff}=\frac{\alpha^2\lambda^2-1}{\Op^2}.
\]
Thus the zero-density condition is
\[
\rho_{\rm eff}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\alpha\lambda|=1.
\]
In the expanding regime \((\lambda>0)\), this becomes
\[
\boxed{\alpha\lambda=1.}
\]
This is the \emph{zero-density threshold}: it answers the cosmological question
\[
\text{Has the expansion become positive effective content rather than empty openness?}
\]

The two thresholds are therefore not the same:
\[
\boxed{\text{Geometry: }\alpha\lambda\Op=1,}
\qquad
\boxed{\text{Cosmology: }\alpha\lambda=1.}
\]
For a large Vessel \((\Op\gg1)\), exact Ricci resonance requires
\[
\lambda=\frac{1}{\alpha\Op},
\]
which is far below the cosmological zero-density threshold
\[
\lambda=\frac{1}{\alpha}.
\]
Consequently, a trajectory may be geometrically Ricci-resonant while still cosmologically
below positive effective density. Conversely, once \(\alpha\lambda>1\), the geometry is typically
super-Ricci for large \(\Op\), since
\[
\xi=\alpha\lambda\Op\gg1.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Different thresholds for different questions]
Ricci resonance measures whether Sophia matches curvature. Zero-density measures whether Sophia
surpasses empty open expansion. Geometry compares Sophia to curvature; Cosmology compares Sophia
to vacuum.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Closed Sophia and Ricci-like Calming of the Vessel}\label{sec:closed-sophia}

In the closed limit there is no continuing Open-Gate replenishment. The balance is
\[
\sigma+\lambda=C_0,
\]
so, for \(\sigma\ge0\), Sophia is bounded:
\[
0\le\lambda\le C_0.
\]
The Brim law is still
\[
\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda,
\]
but closed Sophia has only a finite stock of transmuted resistance. This produces a Ricci-like
\emph{calming} geometry rather than an inexhaustible cosmological expansion.

\subsubsection*{Stabilized closed Sophia}
Suppose
\[
\lambda(t)\to\lambda_\infty>0.
\]
Then
\[
\Op(t)\sim\alpha\lambda_\infty t,
\]
and the spatial curvature satisfies
\[
R_\Sigma(t)=-\frac{2}{\Op(t)^2}
\sim
-\frac{2}{\alpha^2\lambda_\infty^2t^2}.
\]
Thus
\[
R_\Sigma\to0^-.
\]
The Vessel widens into receptive stillness: its live-surface becomes less negatively curved and
more spacious.

This is not exact Ricci flow, because the Ricci-comparison ratio is
\[
\xi=\alpha\lambda\Op
\sim
\alpha^2\lambda_\infty^2t\to\infty.
\]
Therefore stabilized closed Sophia is \emph{Ricci-like by outcome}, but not by exact rate. It is
super-Ricci relative to the intrinsic Ricci-rate, yet it still ends in curvature relaxation.

\subsubsection*{Exact Ricci-resonant closed Sophia}
Exact Ricci resonance requires
\[
\xi=1,
\qquad
\alpha\lambda\Op=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{\alpha\Op(t)}.
\]
Then
\[
\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda=\frac{1}{\Op},
\]
so
\[
\Op\dot\Op=1,
\qquad
\Op(t)^2=\Op(0)^2+2t.
\]
Hence
\[
\Op(t)\sim\sqrt{2t},
\qquad
R_\Sigma(t)\sim-\frac{1}{t}.
\]
This is the exact Ricci-rate for the hyperbolic live-surface. But it requires Sophia to decay
relative to the expanding Brim:
\[
\lambda(t)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha\sqrt{2t}}\to0.
\]

\subsubsection*{Exhausted Sophia and curvature plateau}
If
\[
\lambda(t)\to0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\int_0^\infty\lambda(t)\,dt<\infty,
\]
then
\[
\Op(t)=\Op(0)+\alpha\int_0^t\lambda(s)\,ds
\to\Op^\infty<\infty.
\]
Consequently
\[
R_\Sigma(t)\to-\frac{2}{(\Op^\infty)^2}<0.
\]
This is a stalled Epektasis: the curvature is reduced, but it plateaus at a permanent negative
value.

\begin{corollary}[Closed Sophia as calming]
Closed Sophia is not merely a weaker form of Open-Gate Sophia. It defines a whole Ricci-like
geometry of calming. Exact Ricci resonance occurs only when Sophia decays as
\(1/(\alpha\Op)\). Stabilized bounded Sophia is generically super-Ricci by rate, but still
Ricci-like by outcome: it relaxes curvature and calms the Vessel into receptive stillness.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Area Growth of Receptive Space}\label{sec:area-growth}

Curvature relaxation is not the only geometric effect of Brim expansion. Because
\[
g_\Sigma(t)=\Op(t)^2h_{2D},
\]
the area element of the two-dimensional live-surface scales as
\[
\boxed{dA_\Sigma=\Op^2\,dA_h.}
\]
Taking a logarithmic derivative gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log dA_\Sigma
=
2\frac{\dot\Op}{\Op}.
\]
Using \(\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{\dot{dA}_\Sigma}{dA_\Sigma}
=
2\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\Op}=2H.}
\]
Thus the local receptive area grows at twice the Brim-Hubble rate. Sophia therefore does not
merely relax curvature; it generates receptive area.

The three principal growth regimes are:
\[
\text{Ricci resonance: }\Op(t)\sim\sqrt{2t}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_\Sigma\sim t,
\]
\[
\text{bounded closed Sophia: }\Op(t)\sim t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_\Sigma\sim t^2,
\]
\[
\text{proportional Open-Gate Sophia: }\lambda=\beta\Op
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Op(t)=\Op(0)e^{\alpha\beta t},
\quad
A_\Sigma\sim e^{2\alpha\beta t}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Receptive area]
Curvature measures how tense the Vessel is; area measures how much receptive space it has. Sophia
changes both: it makes the Vessel less curved and more spacious.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Resurrectio as Discrete Curvature Release}\label{sec:discrete-release}

The continuous mode of Epektasis satisfies
\[
\dot R_\Sigma=\frac{4\alpha\lambda}{\Op^3}\ge0
\quad(\lambda\ge0),
\]
so the negative curvature moves continuously toward zero. Resurrectio gives the discrete counterpart.
The reset law is
\[
\Op^+=\Op^-+\kappa_R\Grec^-.
\]
If \(\Grec^->0\), then
\[
\Op^+>\Op^-.
\]
Since
\[
R_\Sigma^-= -\frac{2}{(\Op^-)^2},
\qquad
R_\Sigma^+= -\frac{2}{(\Op^+)^2},
\]
one obtains
\[
\boxed{\Delta R_\Sigma=R_\Sigma^+-R_\Sigma^-
=
2\left[\frac{1}{(\Op^-)^2}-\frac{1}{(\Op^+)^2}\right]>0.}
\]
Thus Resurrectio is a discrete curvature release: it preserves the hyperbolic type of the live-surface
while increasing its curvature radius.

The same jump also expands receptive area:
\[
\frac{dA_\Sigma^+}{dA_\Sigma^-}
=
\left(\frac{\Op^+}{\Op^-}\right)^2>1.
\]
Therefore Resurrectio releases curvature and enlarges receptivity in one discrete act.

\begin{corollary}[Continuous and discrete opening]
Epektasis is continuous curvature relaxation:
\[
\dot R_\Sigma>0.
\]
Resurrectio is discrete curvature release:
\[
\Delta R_\Sigma>0.
\]
Both open the same live-surface toward a larger receptive geometry.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Warp Exponent \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}: Severity of the Live-Domain}\label{sec:warp-p}

The canonical model uses \(p=1\), but the metric naturally allows a family
\[
ds^2=\frac{du^2}{\mu^2}+\mu^{2p}d\Delta^2,
\qquad
\mu=1-\frac{u}{\Op},
\qquad
p>0.
\]
For this family
\[
\boxed{R_\Sigma=-\frac{2p^2}{\Op^2}.}
\]
Thus \(p\) controls the severity of the live-domain near the death-wall. The larger \(p\) is, the
more strongly transverse imbalance is compressed near \(\mu\to0^+\), and the greater the magnitude of
negative curvature.

A useful reading is:
\[
0<p<1:	ext{ soft Vessel geometry},
\]
\[
p=1:	ext{ canonical Vessel geometry},
\]
\[
p>1:	ext{ surgical or severe Vessel geometry}.
\]
The Brim still controls the scale of receptivity:
\[
\Op\uparrow\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_\Sigma\to0^-
\]
for every fixed \(p>0\). Hence even a more severe geometry can be calmed by Brim expansion.

\begin{corollary}[Scale versus severity]
The Brim \(\Op\) controls the scale of receptive space; the warp exponent \(p\) controls the severity
of transverse compression near closure. The canonical V.F.S. geometry remains \(p=1\), while
\(p\neq1\) is best read as an optional family of Vessel geometries.
\end{corollary}


\section{Status of Sophianic Folding inside the Geometric Layer}\label{sec:fold-status}
The following development integrates the separate Sophianic Folding note into the geometric layer.
It should be read as a \textbf{functional-spectral toy theorem} inside the Open-Gate geometry, not as
a full global theorem on the space of all metrics. Its purpose is to test a new geometric regime:
the old surface-form may lose stability while the Brim continues to expand.

The guiding hypothesis is:
\msg{accumulated and growing Sophia can destabilize the old surface-form without decreasing $\Op$.}

\dot\Op>0,\qquad q=0\longrightarrow q\ne0.

Here $q$ is a shape-mode amplitude. The state $q=0$ denotes the old symmetric surface-form;
$q\ne0$ denotes a folded morphology. In later cosmological notation this amplitude may be denoted

\section{Sophianic Folding: Scale versus shape}
The first step is to separate the global Brim-scale from the intrinsic shape of the surface:
\[
 g(t)=\Op(t)^2\,\widehat g(t).
\]
In the ordinary geometric layer, the shape is effectively fixed:
\[
 \widehat g(t)=\widehat g_0.
\]
For Sophianic Folding we allow a shape perturbation:
\[
 \widehat g(t)=\widehat g_0+q(t)\widehat h,
\]
so that
\[
\boxed{
 g(t)=\Op(t)^2\bigl(\widehat g_0+q(t)\widehat h\bigr).
}
\]
The Brim law remains the Open-Gate law
\[
\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda,
\]
so for $\alpha>0$ and $\lambda>0$ the global scale expands. Any collapse below is therefore a
collapse of the old shape branch, not a collapse of the Brim.

\section{Folding energy and the loss of old-form stability}
Let $q(t)$ be the scalar amplitude of a selected folding mode. A minimal Landau-type shape energy is
\[
\mathcal E_{\rm shape}(q;\lambda,\dot\lambda)
=
\frac12\Bigl(a_0-c_0\lambda-c_1\dot\lambda\Bigr)q^2
+
\frac14 b q^4,
\qquad b>0.
\]
The constants have the following roles:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth}L{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Role \\
\midrule
$a_0>0$ & rigidity of the old surface-form \\
$c_0>0$ & influence of accumulated Sophia $\lambda$ on shape instability \\
$c_1>0$ & influence of growing Sophia $\dot\lambda$ on shape instability \\
$b>0$ & nonlinear saturation preventing unbounded folding \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Define the folding index
\[
\boxed{
S(t)=c_0\lambda(t)+c_1\dot\lambda(t)-a_0.
}
\]
Then the quadratic term of the shape energy is negative exactly when $S(t)>0$.
The gradient dynamics is
\[
\dot q=-\frac{\partial\mathcal E_{\rm shape}}{\partial q},
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
\dot q=S(t)q-bq^3.
}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Basic folding threshold]
For fixed $\lambda$ and $\dot\lambda$, the old surface-form $q=0$ is locally stable if
\[
 c_0\lambda+c_1\dot\lambda<a_0,
\]
and unstable if
\[
 c_0\lambda+c_1\dot\lambda>a_0.
\]
When the latter inequality holds, two locally stable folded forms exist:
\[
\boxed{
q_\ast=\pm\sqrt{\frac{c_0\lambda+c_1\dot\lambda-a_0}{b}}.
}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vector field is $f(q)=S q-bq^3$. Its derivative is $f'(q)=S-3bq^2$.
At $q=0$, $f'(0)=S$, so $q=0$ is stable for $S<0$ and unstable for $S>0$.
For $S>0$, the additional equilibria satisfy $q_\ast^2=S/b$. At these equilibria,
$f'(q_\ast)=S-3b(S/b)=-2S<0$, hence they are locally stable.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Growth lowers the folding threshold]
Without the growth term, the static threshold is
\[
\lambda_{\rm fold}^{(0)}=\frac{a_0}{c_0}.
\]
With $\dot\lambda>0$, the effective threshold becomes
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\rm fold}^{\rm eff}
=
\frac{a_0-c_1\dot\lambda}{c_0}.
}
\]
Therefore
\[
\dot\lambda>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_{\rm fold}^{\rm eff}<\lambda_{\rm fold}^{(0)}.
\]
Growing Sophia can therefore trigger folding before accumulated Sophia reaches the static threshold.
\end{corollary}

\section{Functional-spectral formulation of the shape operator}
The scalar variable $q$ can be interpreted as the amplitude of the first unstable eigenmode of the
surface-form. Let $\mathcal M_{\rm shape}$ denote the local space of admissible shape metrics and let

\widehat g=\widehat g_0+h,
\qquad
h\in T_{\widehat g_0}\mathcal M_{\rm shape}.

Assume that the old shape $\widehat g_0$ is a critical point of a shape functional

\mathcal E_{\rm shape}[\widehat g;\lambda,\dot\lambda],
\qquad
\delta\mathcal E_{\rm shape}[\widehat g_0;\lambda,\dot\lambda]=0.

Its second variation is represented by a self-adjoint stability operator:

\boxed{
\delta^2\mathcal E_{\rm shape}[\widehat g_0;\lambda,\dot\lambda](h,h)
=
\langle h,\mathcal L_{\lambda,\dot\lambda}h\rangle_{\widehat g_0}.

One possible inner product is

\langle h,k\rangle_{\widehat g_0}
=
\int_\Sigma \operatorname{tr}_{\widehat g_0}(h\cdot k)\,d\mu_{\widehat g_0}.

The minimal V.F.S. spectral ansatz is

\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\lambda,\dot\lambda}
=
\mathcal L_0-\lambda\mathcal S_0-\dot\lambda\mathcal S_1.
}
\]
Here $\mathcal L_0$ is the old-form rigidity operator, while $\mathcal S_0$ and $\mathcal S_1$
measure how accumulated Sophia and growing Sophia reduce stability of the old form.

Let
\[
\mathcal L_{\lambda,\dot\lambda}e_n
=
\mu_n(\lambda,\dot\lambda)e_n,
\qquad
\mu_1\le \mu_2\le\cdots,

with normalized eigenmodes
\[
\langle e_i,e_j\rangle_{\widehat g_0}=\delta_{ij}.

If

\langle e_1,\mathcal L_0e_1\rangle=a_0,
\qquad
\langle e_1,\mathcal S_0e_1\rangle=c_0,
\qquad
\langle e_1,\mathcal S_1e_1\rangle=c_1,

then
\[
\boxed{
\mu_1(\lambda,\dot\lambda)=a_0-c_0\lambda-c_1\dot\lambda.

Thus the folding condition is the zero-crossing of the first eigenvalue:
\[
\boxed{
\mu_1(\lambda,\dot\lambda)<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c_0\lambda+c_1\dot\lambda>a_0.

Near the first crossing, assume only $e_1$ becomes unstable while $\mu_n>0$ for $n\ge2$.
Then
\[
h=q e_1,
\qquad
\widehat g=\widehat g_0+q e_1,

and the metric becomes

\boxed{
g(t)=\Op(t)^2\bigl(\widehat g_0+q(t)e_1\bigr).

The one-mode energy is

\mathcal E_1(q)
=
\frac12\mu_1 q^2+\frac14bq^4.

For $\mu_1<0$ its stable branches are
\[
\boxed{
\widehat g_\pm
=
\widehat g_0
\pm
\sqrt{\frac{-\mu_1(\lambda,\dot\lambda)}{b}}\,e_1
=
\widehat g_0
\pm
\sqrt{\frac{c_0\lambda+c_1\dot\lambda-a_0}{b}}\,e_1.
}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Spectral single-mode folding]
Assume $\widehat g_0$ is a critical point of $\mathcal E_{\rm shape}$ and the first stability
eigenvalue is $\mu_1=a_0-c_0\lambda-c_1\dot\lambda$. If $\mu_1>0$, the old form is stable in the
first mode. If $\mu_1<0$ and $b>0$, the old branch loses stability and two stable folded branches
appear along $e_1$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Energy-consistent embedding in Sophia dynamics}

Since $b>0$, $c_1>0$, $\eta>0$, and $\lambda\ge0$, one has $B_{\VFS}>0$.

\begin{proof}
The derivative of $\mathcal E_1$ is $-A_{\VFS}q+B_{\VFS}q^3$. Hence the gradient flow is
$\dot q=A_{\VFS}q-B_{\VFS}q^3$. The cubic stability test gives the stated equilibria and their
stability because $B_{\VFS}>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sophia both triggers and limits folding]
The numerator $A_{\VFS}$ is the activation index for folding, while the denominator $B_{\VFS}$

also limiting the amplitude by being embodied into the new form through the term $\eta q^2\lambda$.
\end{corollary}

the one-chart model predicts a folded amplitude beyond the admissible metric range. This does not

chart, a richer shape-mode expansion, or a topology-transition description may be required.

\section{Non-Perelmanian character of Sophianic Folding}
Sophianic Folding should not be identified with a Perelman-type singularity of Ricci flow. In
Perelman's setting, surgery is introduced to remove pathological singular regions that arise under
the evolution of the metric. By contrast, Sophianic Folding is formulated here as a regular
morphological bifurcation of the surface-form.

The transition
\[
q=0\longrightarrow q_\pm\ne0
\]
does not mean that the metric has become singular. It means that the old symmetric branch $q=0$ has
lost dynamical stability, while new folded branches become stable:
\[
q_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}}.
\]
Thus the folding event is not a cutting operation and does not require surgery as long as
\[
\boxed{
0<\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}<q_{\max}^2.
}
\]
The boundary
\[
\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}=q_{\max}^2
\]
should therefore be interpreted not as a Perelmanian singularity, but as the boundary of the current
morphological chart. Beyond this boundary the one-mode ansatz
\[
\widehat g(t)=\widehat g_0+q(t)e_1
\]
may cease to be sufficient. A new geometric chart or a richer shape-mode expansion may be required.

\section{Curvature correction under folding}
The unfurled V.F.S. live surface has scalar curvature
\[
R_0=-\frac{2}{\Op^2}.
\]
Thus global Brim expansion moves the background curvature toward zero. Folding, however, can produce
a local curvature correction. For a symmetric folding mode $q\leftrightarrow -q$, the leading
correction is quadratic:
\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm folded}= -\frac{2}{\Op^2}+\rho_1 q^2+O(q^4).
}
\]
The parameter $\rho_1$ measures the curvature concentration of the first unstable mode $e_1$. The
surface-collapse regime considered here assumes
\[
\rho_1>0.
\]
At the stable folded branch,
\[
q_\ast^2=\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm folded}
=
-\frac{2}{\Op^2}
+
\rho_1\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}
+O(q_\ast^4).
}
\]
Neglecting higher-order corrections and using the energy-consistent denominator,
\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm folded}
=
-\frac{2}{\Op^2}
+
\rho_1
\frac{(c_0-c_1\gamma)\lambda+c_1k_\sigma\sigma+c_1\Igate-a_0}
{b+2c_1\eta\lambda}.
}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Global expansion with local curvature concentration]
Assume $\lambda>0$, $\alpha>0$, $\rho_1>0$, and $A_{\VFS}>0$. Then the Brim expands globally:
\[
\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda>0,
\]
while folding creates a positive curvature correction
\[
\Delta R_{\rm fold}=\rho_1\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}>0.
\]
Thus global expansion and local curvature concentration can occur simultaneously.
\end{proposition}

\section{Positive curvature pockets}
A particularly strong transition occurs when the folding correction overcomes the background negative
curvature. The threshold is

R_{\rm folded}=0.

Using the leading-order expression,

-\frac{2}{\Op^2}+\rho_1\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}=0.

Thus the positive curvature-pocket criterion is

\boxed{

}
\]
This means that an open/hyperbolic background can locally fold into a positive-curvature pocket even
while the global Brim continues to expand.


\section{Lyapunov compatibility: folding as morphological stabilization}
The folding module should not be interpreted as a destabilizing singular event. In the regular
folding domain it is a Lyapunov-compatible relaxation of the surface-form. For fixed V.F.S.
parameters, the one-mode folding energy is
\[
\mathcal E_{\rm fold}(q)
=
-\frac12A_{\VFS}q^2+
\frac14B_{\VFS}q^4,
\qquad B_{\VFS}>0.
\]
The shape dynamics is the gradient flow
\[
\dot q=-\partial_q\mathcal E_{\rm fold}
=A_{\VFS}q-B_{\VFS}q^3.
\]
Therefore, when $A_{\VFS}$ and $B_{\VFS}$ are frozen or vary adiabatically,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal E_{\rm fold}(q)
=
-\left(\partial_q\mathcal E_{\rm fold}\right)^2
\le 0.
}
\]
Equivalently, since $\dot q=-\partial_q\mathcal E_{\rm fold}$,
\[
\boxed{
\dot{\mathcal E}_{\rm fold}=-\dot q^{\,2}\le0.
}
\]
Thus folding is an energy-descending process: the old form does not explode into a singularity, but
relaxes toward a lower-energy morphology.

The stable folded branches have energy
\[
\mathcal E_{\rm fold}(q_\pm)
=-\frac{A_{\VFS}^2}{4B_{\VFS}}<0
\qquad (A_{\VFS}>0),
\]
while the old branch has
\[
\mathcal E_{\rm fold}(0)=0.
\]
Hence the folded morphology is energetically preferred once $A_{\VFS}>0$.

To write the folding contribution as a non-negative Lyapunov component, shift the energy by its
minimum. For $A_{\VFS}>0$ define
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_{\rm fold}
=
\mathcal E_{\rm fold}-\mathcal E_{{\rm fold},\min}
=
\frac{B_{\VFS}}{4}
\left(q^2-\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}\right)^2
\ge0.
}
\]
For $A_{\VFS}<0$, the minimum is at $q=0$ and one may use
\[
\mathcal V_{\rm fold}
=
\frac12|A_{\VFS}|q^2+
\frac14B_{\VFS}q^4.
\]
A natural extended Lyapunov candidate is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm ext}
=
\mathcal L_{\VFS}+w_q\mathcal V_{\rm fold},
\qquad w_q>0.
}
\]
If the base Open-Gate Lyapunov functional satisfies
\[
\dot{\mathcal L}_{\VFS}\le C-C_1\mathcal L_{\VFS},
\qquad C_1>0,
\]
and if $\mathcal V_{\rm fold}$ remains bounded in the regular folding domain, then
\[
\boxed{
\dot{\mathcal L}_{\rm ext}
\le
C_{\rm ext}-C_1\mathcal L_{\rm ext}
}
\]
for a modified constant $C_{\rm ext}$. Thus the folding extension preserves dissipative boundedness.

When the parameters move, one has
\[
\dot{\mathcal E}_{\rm fold}
=
-\left(\partial_q\mathcal E_{\rm fold}\right)^2
-\frac12q^2\dot A_{\VFS}
+\frac14q^4\dot B_{\VFS}.
\]
The first term is strictly dissipative. The last two terms represent slow variation of the V.F.S.
background. Hence the compatibility statement is adiabatic: if $|\dot A_{\VFS}|+|\dot B_{\VFS}|$ is
bounded relative to the shape-relaxation rate, the folding module remains Lyapunov-compatible.

\centering
In the regular domain, Sophianic Folding is morphological stabilization: an obsolete form relaxes
into a lower-energy folded morphology while the Brim continues to expand.
}}
\end{center}

\section{Coupling back to the V.F.S. core}
The folding module is not an isolated geometric add-on. It can be coupled back into the V.F.S.
core as a feedback mechanism acting on the accumulation of Sophia. In the minimal coupled model,
the basic synergistic intensity and Brim-flow remain
\[
u=\sqrt{VF+\varepsilon},
\qquad
\dot\Op=\alpha\lambda.
\]
The new ingredient is that the shape-mode $q$ feeds back into the Sophia-flow:
\[
\boxed{
\dot\lambda
=
k_\sigma\sigma+
\Igate
-\gamma\lambda
-\eta q^2\lambda.
}
\]
Here the term $-\eta q^2\lambda$ should not be read as the destruction of Sophia. It represents the
embodiment of excess Sophia into morphology: the accumulated noetic pressure becomes a stable
shape-deformation of the surface-form.

The coupled shape equation remains
\[
\boxed{
\dot q=A_{\VFS}q-B_{\VFS}q^3,
}
\]
with
\[
A_{\VFS}
=
(c_0-c_1\gamma)\lambda+c_1k_\sigma\sigma+c_1\Igate-a_0,
\qquad
B_{\VFS}=b+2c_1\eta\lambda.
\]
Thus the core produces Sophia, Sophia activates folding, and folding regulates the further
accumulation of Sophia.

Let
\[
J_{\rm in}=k_\sigma\sigma+\Igate.
\]
Before folding, $q=0$, so
\[
\dot\lambda_{\rm before}=J_{\rm in}-\gamma\lambda.
\]
In the regular folded regime,
\[
q_\ast^2=Q_{\rm fold}=\frac{A_{\VFS}}{B_{\VFS}}>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dot\lambda_{\rm after}
=
J_{\rm in}-\gamma\lambda-\eta Q_{\rm fold}\lambda.
\]
The folding correction to the Sophia-flow is
\[
\boxed{
\Delta\dot\lambda_{\rm fold}
=
\dot\lambda_{\rm after}-\dot\lambda_{\rm before}
=
-\eta\lambda Q_{\rm fold}.
}
\]
Hence, whenever $\lambda>0$, $\eta>0$, and $Q_{\rm fold}>0$,
\[
\boxed{
\Delta\dot\lambda_{\rm fold}<0.
}
\]
Regular Sophianic Folding therefore lowers the rate of further Sophia accumulation. It stabilizes not
only the surface-form, but also the tempo of Sophianic excess.

Equivalently, inside the folded branch the Sophia equation can be written as
\[
\dot\lambda
=J_{\rm in}-(\gamma+\eta Q_{\rm fold})\lambda.
\]
This motivates the effective damping coefficient
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm eff}=\gamma+\eta Q_{\rm fold}.
}
\]
In the regular folded regime,
\[
Q_{\rm fold}>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gamma_{\rm eff}>\gamma.
\]
Thus folding acts as a negative feedback loop:
\[
\lambda\uparrow
\Longrightarrow
A_{\VFS}\uparrow
\Longrightarrow
Q_{\rm fold}>0
\Longrightarrow
q^2\uparrow
\Longrightarrow
\gamma_{\rm eff}\uparrow
\Longrightarrow
\dot\lambda\downarrow.
\]
The stationary Sophia level is also lowered by folding. If $J_{\rm in}$ is treated as fixed, then
\[
\dot\lambda=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_\ast=\frac{J_{\rm in}}{\gamma+\eta q^2}.
\]
Before folding,
\[
\lambda_{\rm old}=\frac{J_{\rm in}}{\gamma}.
\]
After folding, since $q^2>0$,
\[
\lambda_{\rm fold}=\frac{J_{\rm in}}{\gamma+\eta q^2}
<
\frac{J_{\rm in}}{\gamma}
=
\lambda_{\rm old}.
\]
So regular folding reduces the equilibrium level of unembodied Sophia.

This gives the core-coupling lemma.
