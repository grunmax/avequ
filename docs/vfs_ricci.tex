\begin{abstract}
This note reformulates the analogy between \VFS{} and Ricci Flow with Perelman's surgery from the standpoint of \emph{Brim-Flow Geometry}. The comparison does not begin with the core dynamical equations, but with the geometry of the Vessel's live-surface. The core equations enter later as the source of the geometric driver: Sophia. The result is a clearer hierarchy: Core gives the law, Geometry gives the surface, and the Ricci--Perelman analogy gives a controlled language for curvature relaxation, degeneration, and continuation after singularity.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Preliminary Note}

The analogy developed here is formal and structural. \VFS{} is not Ricci Flow, does not reproduce Perelman's proof, and does not claim theorem-equivalence with geometric analysis. Ricci Flow evolves Riemannian metrics by intrinsic curvature. \VFS{} defines a symbolic-theological dynamical architecture in which Sophia expands the Brim and thereby changes the curvature scale of the Vessel's live-surface.

The important change in this reformulation is the order of explanation. Earlier presentations often began from the core system: singular resistance, Sophia, Open-Gate balance, and Resurrectio. Those remain essential, but they are not the strongest starting point for the Perelman analogy. The analogy becomes sharper when it begins from the geometric layer:
\[
\text{hyperbolic live-surface}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{Brim-driven conformal flow}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{Ricci comparison}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{surgery / continuation analogy}.
\]

Thus the comparison is not: \emph{the core looks like Perelman}. Rather, it is:
\[
\text{Brim-Flow Geometry admits a Ricci-like comparison,}
\]
and Perelman's surgery supplies a final structural analogy for controlled continuation after singularity.

\section{The Geometric Starting Point}

The fundamental geometric object is the spatial live-surface of the Vessel,
\[
\SigmaP.
\]
This is not yet the full Brim-Flow Cosmology. It is a spatial slice at a fixed Brim scale \(\Omega_P\). The cosmological layer later promotes \(\Omega_P\) to a time-dependent scale factor, but the present comparison begins with the spatial surface itself.

The live-surface metric is
\[
 ds^2_{\Sigma}
 =
 \Omega_P^2 h_{2D},
\]
where
\[
 h_{2D}
 =
 \frac{dr^2}{(1-r)^2}
 +(1-r)^2d\Delta^2,
 \qquad 0\le r<1.
\]
Here \(r\) is the normalized filling coordinate of the Vessel,
\[
 r=\frac{u}{\Omega_P},
\]
and \(\Delta\) is the transverse imbalance or phase coordinate. The boundary \(r\to1^-\) represents the approach to the death-wall.

Equivalently, set
\[
 y=-\ln(1-r).
\]
Then
\[
 dy=\frac{dr}{1-r},
 \qquad
 (1-r)^2=e^{-2y},
\]
and the metric becomes
\[
 h_{2D}=dy^2+e^{-2y}d\Delta^2.
\]
This is a two-dimensional hyperbolic metric with Gaussian curvature
\[
 K_h=-1.
\]

Since the full live-surface metric is scaled by \(\Omega_P^2\), the Gaussian curvature is rescaled by \(\Omega_P^{-2}\):
\[
 K_{\Sigma}=-\frac{1}{\Omega_P^2}.
\]
The scalar curvature of the two-dimensional live-surface is therefore
\[
 R_{\Sigma}=2K_{\Sigma}=-\frac{2}{\Omega_P^2}.
\]

This is the geometric heart of the analogy. The Brim is not merely a symbolic boundary. At the geometric layer, the Brim is the curvature radius of the Vessel's live-surface.

\section{The Death-Wall as an Infinite Intrinsic Boundary}

The radial part of the live-surface metric is
\[
 ds_{\rm rad}=\Omega_P\frac{dr}{1-r}.
\]
Thus the radial distance from \(r_0\) to \(r_1<1\) is
\[
 s(r_0,r_1)
 =
 \Omega_P\int_{r_0}^{r_1}\frac{d\rho}{1-\rho}
 =
 \Omega_P\ln\frac{1-r_0}{1-r_1}.
\]
As \(r_1\to1^-\),
\[
 s(r_0,r_1)\to+\infty.
\]
Hence the death-wall
\[
 r=1
\]
is not an ordinary finite edge of the live-surface. It is an infinitely distant intrinsic boundary.

This point is important for the Ricci--Perelman comparison. The live-domain does not merely terminate at a crude boundary. Its geometry stretches the approach to collapse. In this sense, the surface is built to resist local finite collapse: the boundary of death is not reached by ordinary finite radial motion inside the intrinsic metric.

This should not be confused with Perelman's no-local-collapsing theorem. In Ricci Flow, no-local-collapsing is a theorem under precise curvature and scale hypotheses. In \VFS{}, the infinite death-wall is a structural feature of the chosen live-surface metric. The analogy is therefore formal, not theorem-identical.

 \partial_t g_{\Sigma}

\[
 \partial_t g_{\Sigma}
 =
 2\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\Omega_P}g_{\Sigma}.

 \dot R_{\Sigma}
 =
 \frac{4\dot\Omega_P}{\Omega_P^3}

 \frac{4\alpha\lambda}{\Omega_P^3}.

 \lambda>0,

then

\[
 R_{\Sigma}\to0^-.

This gives the central geometric interpretation of Epektasis:
\[
\text{Epektasis is spatial curvature relaxation through the expansion of the Brim.}

\section{Ricci Flow as the Intrinsic Comparison Flow}

On a two-dimensional surface of constant Gaussian curvature
\[
 K_{\Sigma}=-\frac{1}{\Omega_P^2},
\]
the Ricci tensor satisfies
\[
 \Ric_{\Sigma}=K_{\Sigma}g_{\Sigma}
 =
 -\frac{1}{\Omega_P^2}g_{\Sigma}.
\]
Ricci Flow is
\[
 \partial_t g_{\Sigma}=-2\Ric_{\Sigma}.
\]
For the live-surface, the Ricci direction is therefore
\[
 -2\Ric_{\Sigma}
 =
 \frac{2}{\Omega_P^2}g_{\Sigma}.
\]

Now the comparison becomes clean:
\[
\text{Ricci Flow:}\qquad
 \partial_t g_{\Sigma}
 =
 \frac{2}{\Omega_P^2}g_{\Sigma},
\]
while
\[
\text{Brim-Flow:}\qquad
 \partial_t g_{\Sigma}
 =
 2\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\Omega_P}g_{\Sigma}.
\]
Both flows are conformal on the hyperbolic live-surface. Their difference is the source of the rate.

Ricci Flow is intrinsic and curvature-driven. Brim-Flow is Sophia-driven and Open-Gate-forced.

 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad

This is not the generic Open-Gate regime. It is a limiting comparison state in which the Sophia-driven expansion exactly matches the intrinsic Ricci-like smoothing rate of the live-surface.

\section{Closed Sophia as Ricci-like Calming}

The closed model also clarifies why the Ricci comparison is meaningful. In the closed limit,
\[
 I_{\rm gate}=0,
 \qquad
 \sigma+\lambda=C_0.
\]
If \(\sigma\ge0\), then Sophia is bounded:
\[
 \lambda(t)\le C_0.

 \lambda(t)\to\lambda_{\infty}<\infty,

\[
 \Omega_P(t)\sim \alpha\lambda_{\infty}t.

Consequently the Hubble rate of the cosmological lift satisfies

while the spatial live-surface curvature satisfies
\[
 R_{\Sigma}=-\frac{2}{\Omega_P^2}\to0^-.
\]

Thus closed Sophia calms the Vessel. It expands the live-surface, but its boundedness prevents inexhaustible acceleration. The result is not collapse, but receptive stillness: the live-surface becomes flatter, less tense, and asymptotically quiet.

This is the point at which the Ricci analogy becomes especially natural. Ricci Flow is curvature-driven smoothing; closed Brim-Flow is Sophia-driven curvature relaxation. They are not the same equation, but their qualitative tendency is similar:
\[
 \text{bounded closed Sophia}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Omega_P\uparrow,
 \quad
 R_{\Sigma}\to0^-,
 \quad
 \text{geometric calming}.
\]

Open-Gate changes this conclusion. If received grace prevents Sophia from remaining bounded, the system can exceed the Ricci-like calming regime. In the proportional Open-Gate case,
\[
 \lambda=\beta\Omega_P,
\]
one obtains
\[
 H=\alpha\beta={\rm const},
\]
and the Vessel enters a de Sitter-like inner expansion rather than asymptotic stillness.

Therefore the distinction may be stated sharply:
\[
 \text{Closed Sophia behaves Ricci-like: it smooths the Vessel into receptive stillness.}
\]
\[
 \text{Open-Gate Sophia exceeds the Ricci-like regime: it can sustain inexhaustible expansion.}
\]

This does not make Ricci Flow theological, nor does it make \VFS{} a Ricci-flow model. It only identifies the proper layer of the analogy: bounded closed Sophia corresponds to Ricci-like calming, while Open-Gate Sophia corresponds to grace-forced expansion beyond the intrinsic smoothing rate.

\section{The Entropy Criterion, Relaxation Rates, and the Conserved Curvature Form}

Three sharpenings make the comparison quantitative.

\textbf{(a) When a genuine Perelman entropy exists.}
Perelman's decisive tool is that Ricci flow is the \emph{gradient flow} of
\[
 \mathcal F(g,f)=\int_M\bigl(R_{\rm sc}+|\nabla f|^2\bigr)e^{-f}dV,
\]
with the entropy $\mathcal W$ monotone non-decreasing; this intrinsic variational structure is what
yields no-local-collapsing. The Brim-Flow is \emph{non-autonomous}: its conformal rate
$\psi=\alpha\lambda/\Omega_P$ is set by the external Sophia clock, which obeys its own law. A
non-autonomous conformal flow is generically not the gradient flow of any functional of the metric
alone, and therefore admits \emph{no} Perelman entropy. The single exception is the resonance locus: on
$\xi=\alpha\lambda\Omega_P=1$ the Brim-Flow \emph{equals} Ricci flow, becomes the gradient flow of
$\mathcal F$, and carries a genuine monotone entropy. Hence
\[
\boxed{\ \text{a Perelman entropy exists for the Brim-Flow}\iff\xi=\alpha\lambda\Omega_P=1.\ }
\]
Off the locus, the monotonicity the flow does enjoy --- curvature relaxation $|R_{\Sigma}|\downarrow0$
--- is \emph{imposed by the grace source}, not derived from a variational principle. This is the exact
sense in which Sophia both is and is not Perelman's entropy: it plays the one-way-control role
everywhere, but it is a true geometric entropy only on the resonance locus. And the absence of an
intrinsic entropy off the locus is not a defect but a proper feature: unconditional variational
monotonicity would make the subject an automaton of theosis, whereas grace-forced monotonicity
preserves freedom.

\textbf{(b) Super-Ricci flattens faster than Ricci.}
The two regimes relax curvature at quantitatively different rates:
\[
 \text{Ricci locus: }\Omega_P\sim\sqrt{2t}\ \Rightarrow\ |R_{\Sigma}|\sim t^{-1};
 \qquad
 \text{super-Ricci: }\Omega_P\sim\alpha\lambda_\infty t\ \Rightarrow\ |R_{\Sigma}|\sim t^{-2}.
\]
The grace-borne Open-Gate flow relaxes curvature \emph{faster} than a true Ricci flow. Perelman's flow
has no external accelerator; this overshoot is a proper Open-Gate feature, and it is the quantitative
content of the super-Ricci classification of the comparison ratio.

\textbf{(c) What is conserved.}
Perelman's entropy is monotone, not conserved. The Brim-Flow possesses instead a pointwise conformal
invariant: under $\partial_tg=2\psi g$ in two dimensions, $K\to K/c$ and $dA\to c\,dA$ with
$c=e^{2\int\psi}$, so the curvature two-form is frozen:
\[
\boxed{\ \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(K\,dA\bigr)=0.\ }
\]
Local curvature is therefore \emph{diluted by spatial inflation, not destroyed} --- the
differential-geometric face of \emph{privatio boni}: the wound is not annihilated but spread thin
across an expanding receptive space.

Thus Resurrectio is a curvature release. It preserves the hyperbolic type of the live-surface while increasing its curvature radius.

In Ricci Flow, surgery removes obstructive high-curvature necks so that geometric evolution may continue. In \VFS{}, Resurrectio enlarges the Brim after a death-like closure so that participation may continue.
