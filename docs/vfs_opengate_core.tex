\section{Synergic Intensity, Threshold, and Transmissive Coordinate}
\sub{Local synergy}
The operational synergic intensity is the geometric mean of Will and Action, kept strictly positive by the Manere seed \(\varepsilon\):
\[
u(t)=\sqrt{V(t)F(t)+\varepsilon}.
\]
This is the local synergic intensity: will and action must meet. The seed \(\varepsilon\) prevents
collapse of the expression into a singular zero.
\plainnote{Will and Action only count when they pull together: this product-root is large only if BOTH are sizeable, and collapses toward zero if either one does. The tiny \(\varepsilon\) just keeps it from being exactly zero.}

\sub{Law of Human Balance identity}
Its growth rate is the symmetric average of the two faculties' contributions, so neither Will nor Action alone can drive \(u\):
\[
\dot u=\frac{V'F+VF'}{2\sqrt{VF+\varepsilon}}=\frac12\Bigl(\frac{V'F}{u}+\frac{F'V}{u}\Bigr).
\]
This identity shows how the growth of the shared will--action coordinate is weighted symmetrically
by the other pole. The formula is an identity; the existential law is that will and action become
strongest when neither pole is isolated.
\plainnote{How fast synergy grows is the balanced blend of how fast Will and Action grow --- pushing only one gives diminishing returns; real growth needs both moving.}

\sub{Critical threshold} \(\Lambda_c=\gamma/\delta\). This separates Collapse, Stasis, and
Transformation.

\sub{Transmissive coordinate}
The transmissive state measures synergy net of singular resistance; its sign decides whether light is withheld or passes:
\[
x(t)=u(t)-\sigma(t)=\sqrt{V(t)F(t)+\varepsilon}-\sigma(t).
\]
This is the coordinate seen by Filtrum Lucis: synergy minus resistance.
\plainnote{\(x\) is the single number that governs everything downstream: synergy minus resistance. \(x>0\) means light gets through; \(x<0\) means resistance still blocks it.}
\mathnote{\(u=\sqrt{VF+\varepsilon}\) is a regularised \emph{geometric mean} of \(V,F\): it is small unless \emph{both} poles are appreciable (an AND-coupling, not a sum), and the seed \(\varepsilon>0\) keeps it Lipschitz at \(V=F=0\), removing the square-root singularity. The scalar \(x=u-\sigma\) is the one-dimensional reduced coordinate onto which the filter \(\Phi\) acts.}
\padd{\pflag\ \textbf{Two levels of synergy.} \(u\) is the \emph{operational} synergic intensity
--- the transmissive potency that drives \(\dot\sigma\); the Gratia-Synergica response \Syn\ (\S6)
is a distinct quantity, \(u\neq\Syn\). \textbf{Three modes of \(x\):} \(x<0\) \(\sigma\)-dominance;
\(x=0\) threshold (\(u=\sigma\)); \(x>0\) post-resistance access. \emph{Caveat:} \(x\) is not a
degree of holiness and not a moral or ontological verdict --- only local transmissive
accessibility.}

\section{Resistance Dynamics and Three Regimes}
\sub{Resistance equation}
Singular resistance grows below the threshold and decays above it, gated by \(\tanh(\kappa\sigma)\) so that \(\sigma=0\) stays protected:
\[
\frac{d\sigma}{dt}=(\gamma-\delta u)\tanh(\kappa\sigma)=(\gamma-\delta\sqrt{VF+\varepsilon})\tanh(\kappa\sigma).
\]
\plainnote{Below the threshold hardship \(\sigma\) grows; above it, it melts away. The \(\tanh\) factor makes \(\sigma=0\) a floor you cannot fall through --- once cleansed, it stays cleansed.}
\sub{Protection of \(\sigma\ge0\):} \(\sigma(0)\ge0\Rightarrow\sigma(t)\ge0\ \forall t\ge0\). The
factor \(\tanh(\kappa\sigma)\) makes \(\sigma=0\) a protected boundary.
\mathnote{Since \(\tanh(\kappa\sigma)\to0\) as \(\sigma\to0\), the line \(\sigma=0\) carries zero flux and is an \emph{invariant boundary}; hence \(\{\sigma\ge0\}\) is forward-invariant. The bracket \((\gamma-\delta u)\) is the bifurcation parameter: for \(u>\Lambda_c\) the boundary is attracting (resistance decays), for \(u<\Lambda_c\) repelling; \(u=\Lambda_c\) is the transcritical threshold.}

\sub{Equilibria of resistance:} \(\dot\sigma=0\iff\sigma=0\) or \(u=\Lambda_c\).

\sub{Three regimes:} \(u<\Lambda_c\) Collapse; \(u=\Lambda_c\) Stasis /
\(\chi\lambda\iota\alpha\rho\acute o\varsigma\); \(u>\Lambda_c\) Transformation.
\plainnote{Three fates, set by synergy versus threshold: too little leads to collapse, exactly at it to lukewarm stasis, enough to genuine transformation.}

\sub{Manere sub-criticality:} \(0<\varepsilon<\Lambda_c^2=(\gamma/\delta)^2\). The Manere seed
keeps the denominator alive, but it must remain sub-critical: the passive branch \(V=F=0\) should
not already count as Transformation.

\sub{Softplus filter}
The filter of light is a smooth, strictly positive softplus with curvature \(k/4\) at the origin:
\[
\Phi(x)=\tfrac1k\ln(1+e^{kx}),\qquad \Phi(x)>0,\qquad \Phi''(0)=\tfrac{k}{4}.
\]
Filtrum Lucis is the smooth light-filter. It is softplus-like; its finite positivity is the formal
trace of \emph{Lux in Tenebris}.
\plainnote{A smooth ramp: for very negative \(x\) it outputs almost nothing; for positive \(x\) it rises like a line. No sharp corner anywhere --- that smoothness is what lets every later derivative exist.}
\padd{\pflag\ John~1:5 --- the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it:
\(\Phi(x)>0\) for all \(x\).}

\sub{Sigmoid transmissivity}
Its derivative is the logistic transmissivity --- the fraction of light passed --- rising from \(0\) through \(\tfrac12\) at threshold to \(1\):
\[
T_F(x)=\Phi'(x)=\frac1{1+e^{-kx}},\qquad 0<T_F<1,\ \ T_F(-\infty)=0,\ T_F(0)=\tfrac12,\ T_F(+\infty)=1.
\]
The filter itself is \(\Phi\); its transmissivity \(T_F\) is sigmoid-shaped.
\plainnote{The S-curve says what fraction of light passes: \(0\) when blocked, \(1\) when fully open, exactly one half at the threshold. It is the dimmer switch of the filter.}

\sub{Higher derivatives of Filtrum Lucis}
Each higher derivative factors through the logistic as a polynomial \(Q_n\) in \(T_F\) scaled by \(k^{n-1}\):
\begin{align*}
\Phi''(x)&=kT_F(1-T_F),\\
\Phi'''(x)&=k^2T_F(1-T_F)(1-2T_F),\\
\Phi^{(4)}(x)&=k^3T_F(1-T_F)(1-6T_F+6T_F^2),\\
\Phi^{(n)}(x)&=k^{n-1}Q_n(T_F(x)).
\end{align*}
These derivatives feed the differential ladder of Pleroma.
\plainnote{Every higher rate-of-change of the filter is the same S-curve times a simple polynomial. That clean pattern is why the Cross and the two Anastasis gates appear later as neat zeros.}
\mathnote{\(\Phi\) is the \emph{softplus}, a smooth approximation of \(\max(0,x)\) (ReLU), exact as \(k\to\infty\). Its derivative \(T_F\) is the logistic sigmoid and \(\Phi''=kT_F(1-T_F)\) is the logistic ``bell'', so \(\Phi\) is convex everywhere. The pattern \(\Phi^{(n)}=k^{n-1}Q_n(T_F)\) packages every higher derivative as a polynomial \(Q_n\) in \(T_F\); the roots of \(Q_3,Q_4\) give the Paschal nodes below.}

\[
P(t)=E_0+\Syn(t)+\Phi(x(t)),\qquad P(t)\ge E_0.

\[
P_{\rm full}=\text{Imago Dei}+\text{Gratia Synergica}+\text{Transmissive Passage}.

\plainnote{Being \(P\) is three things added up: a fixed floor \(E_0\) (the image of God you start with), grace that saturates, and filtered light that can grow without limit. So \(P\) never drops below \(E_0\).}

\section{The Cathartic Neck}
This section establishes the Cathartic Neck of \S10 as a theorem. The exact curvature identity
splits \(\ddot x\) into a slow \emph{expansion} \(\Upsilon\) and a fast \emph{compression}
\(\Xi=\kappa r^2\psi(T)\); from the unimodality of \(\psi\) follow uniqueness, an explicit fold
threshold in \(\kappa\), the neck's migration to the cleansing boundary, a nested Fenichel reduction
of the full system, and the bridge by which Katharsis ignites Sophianic Folding (\S13).
\plainnote{In plain words. The neck is the narrowest point of the whole passage --- the instant just before breakthrough, where hardship is deepest and \(\sigma\) is about to vanish. This section proves such a bottleneck must exist: it is a real fold, the mathematical shape of a turning point, not an accident of the picture.}
\emph{Section-local notation:} \(T:=\tanh(\kappa\sigma)\), \(r:=\delta u-\gamma>0\) (Transformation),
\(\psi(T):=T(1-T^2)\), \(\Upsilon\) (slow expansion), \(\Xi\) (fast compression); these are confined
to this section and are distinct from Gignesthai \(S\) (\S7), the resistance opacity \(\phi\) (\S2),
the transmissivity \(T_F\) (\S5), and the balance constants \(C_\bullet\).

\begin{definition}[Cathartic neck]
A \emph{neck} is a time \(t_c\) with \(\ddot x(t_c)=0\) and \(\dddot x(t_c)>0\): the upward curvature
crossing, Odinai (\(\ddot x<0\)) giving way to Anastasis (\(\ddot x>0\)).
\end{definition}
On the passage interval \(I=[t_0,t_1]\) we assume:
\begin{hyp}[Passage]\label{nk:HP}\(u\in C^3(I)\); \(T\) strictly decreasing from \(T_0\), \(T\to0\).\end{hyp}
\begin{hyp}[Transformation]\label{nk:HT}\(r(t)\in[r_*,\bar r]\), \(r_*>0\).\end{hyp}
\begin{hyp}[Positive demand]\label{nk:HD}\(\Upsilon(t):=\ddot u+\delta\dot u\,T\in[\underline\Upsilon,\bar\Upsilon]\), \(\underline\Upsilon>0\); \(\ddot u(t_1)>0\).\end{hyp}
\begin{hyp}[Interior]\label{nk:HI}\(\mu:=1-u/\Omega_P\ge\mu_*>0\) on \(I\) \textup{(used in the Fenichel reduction only)}.\end{hyp}

\subsection*{The exact curvature identity}
\begin{lemma}[Fast/slow splitting]\label{nk:id}
Along any \(C^2\) solution,
\[
\ddot\sigma=-\delta\dot u\,T+\kappa\,r^2\,T(1-T^2),\qquad
\boxed{\ \ddot x=\underbrace{\ddot u+\delta\dot u\,T}_{\Upsilon}\;-\;\underbrace{\kappa\,r^2\,\psi(T)}_{\Xi\ \ge0},\quad \psi(T)=T(1-T^2).\ }
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With \(\dot\sigma=g\,T\), \(g=\gamma-\delta u=-r\): \(\ddot\sigma=g'\dot u\,T+g\,\kappa(1-T^2)\,gT
=-\delta\dot u\,T+\kappa g^2T(1-T^2)\); then \(\ddot x=\ddot u-\ddot\sigma\), \(g^2=r^2\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The compression profile and \texorpdfstring{\(\kappa\)}{kappa}-uniform uniqueness}
\begin{lemma}[Unimodality]\label{nk:psi}
\(\psi(T)=T-T^3\) has \(\psi(0)=\psi(1)=0\), \(\psi>0\) on \((0,1)\), \(\psi'=1-3T^2\); it increases
on \([0,T^*]\), decreases on \([T^*,1]\), with \(T^*=1/\sqrt3\) and
\(\psi_*:=\psi(T^*)=\tfrac{2\sqrt3}{9}\approx0.3849\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness, every \(\kappa\)]\label{nk:uniq}
Under \textsc{\ref{nk:HP}--\ref{nk:HD}}, for every \(\kappa>0\) there is at most one cathartic neck on
\(I\); when it exists it is nondegenerate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A neck is where \(\Xi-\Upsilon\) crosses \(0\) downward (as \(t\uparrow\), \(T\downarrow\)). Since
\(T\) sweeps \([0,T_0]\) monotonically and \(\psi\) is unimodal, \(T\) passes \(T^*\) at most once.
With \(\dot T<0\): on \(T<T^*\), \(\psi'>0\Rightarrow\dot\Xi<0\) (up to the slow \(\tfrac{d(r^2)}{dt}\)
term), so \(\Xi-\Upsilon\) decreases and crosses \(0\) downward at most once; on \(T>T^*\),
\(\psi'<0\Rightarrow\dot\Xi>0\), so any zero there is an upward crossing of \(\Xi-\Upsilon\) (a
pre-compression, not a neck). Hence at most one neck, on \(T<T^*\); there
\(\dddot x=\dot\Upsilon-\dot\Xi=\dot\Upsilon+|\dot\Xi|>0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Existence: the fold bifurcation in \texorpdfstring{\(\kappa\)}{kappa}}
\begin{theorem}[Fold threshold]\label{nk:fold}
Under \textsc{\ref{nk:HP}--\ref{nk:HD}}: \textup{(a)} if \(T_0\ge T^*\) and \(\kappa r_*^2\psi_*>\bar\Upsilon\)
a unique neck exists; \textup{(b)} if \(\kappa\bar r^2\psi_*<\underline\Upsilon\) then \(\ddot x>0\)
on \(I\) (no neck); \textup{(c)} hence
\(\kappa_{\rm crit}\in[\underline\Upsilon/(\bar r^2\psi_*),\,\bar\Upsilon/(r_*^2\psi_*)]\), and for
constant \(r,\Upsilon\),
\[
\boxed{\ \kappa_{\rm crit}=\frac{\Upsilon}{r^2\psi_*}=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\,\frac{\Upsilon}{r^2}\approx2.598\,\frac{\Upsilon}{r^2}\ }.
\]
At \(\kappa=\kappa_{\rm crit}\) the crossing pair is born tangentially at \(T=T^*\): a fold
(saddle--node) of the zero set of \(\ddot x\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(b) \(\Xi=\kappa r^2\psi(T)\le\kappa\bar r^2\psi_*<\underline\Upsilon\le\Upsilon\Rightarrow\ddot x>0\).
(a) At \(T=T^*\le T_0\), \(\Xi\ge\kappa r_*^2\psi_*>\bar\Upsilon\ge\Upsilon\Rightarrow\ddot x<0\); as
\(T\to0\), \(\Xi\to0\Rightarrow\ddot x>0\); Theorem~\ref{nk:uniq} gives one upward crossing. (c) The
bounds bracket the threshold and coincide for constant coefficients at \(\kappa r^2\psi_*=\Upsilon\);
there \(\max_T(\Xi-\Upsilon)=0\) only at \(T^*\), a tangency.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Birth and migration; localization}
\begin{theorem}[Migration \texorpdfstring{\(T^*\to0\)}{}]\label{nk:mig}
For \(\kappa>\kappa_{\rm crit}\) (constant coefficients) the neck is the smaller root \(T_c\in(0,T^*)\)
of \(\kappa r^2\psi(T)=\Upsilon\); as \(\kappa\downarrow\kappa_{\rm crit}\), \(T_c\uparrow1/\sqrt3\),
and as \(\kappa\to\infty\),
\[
T_c=\frac{\Upsilon}{\kappa r^2}\bigl(1+O(\kappa^{-2})\bigr)\downarrow0,\qquad
\sigma_c=\frac{\Upsilon}{\kappa^2r^2}\bigl(1+O(\kappa^{-2})\bigr)=O(\kappa^{-2}),
\]
so \(x_c=u(t_c)+O(\kappa^{-2})\). With \(\Upsilon\to\ddot u\) at small \(T\) this is the sharp-regime
localization \(T_c\approx\ddot u/(\kappa r^2)\): \textbf{the neck is universally the cleansing
boundary \(\sigma\to0\).}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On \(T<T^*\), \(\psi\) is increasing, so \(\kappa r^2\psi(T)=\Upsilon\) has a unique root, equal to
\(T^*\) at \(\kappa_{\rm crit}\) and decreasing in \(\kappa\). For small \(T_c\),
\(\psi(T_c)=T_c+O(T_c^3)\Rightarrow T_c=\Upsilon/(\kappa r^2)+O(\kappa^{-3})\), and
\(\operatorname{artanh}T_c=T_c+O(T_c^3)\) gives \(\sigma_c\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The Fenichel slow manifold of the full system}
In the sharp regime the neck sits on a slow manifold of the full coupled core. With \(\epsilon=1/\kappa\),
\(\eta=\kappa\sigma\), the resistance is fast and \(y=(V,F,\Omega_P,\Grec)\) slow (\(\lambda\) slaved

\begin{theorem}[Fenichel I; Pleroma~Christi reduction]\label{nk:fen}
Under \textsc{\ref{nk:HT}--\ref{nk:HI}}, for \(\kappa\) large there is a locally invariant attracting
slow manifold \(\Mk=\{\sigma=h_\kappa(y)\}\), \(h_\kappa=O(\kappa^{-2})\), contracting at rate

\[
\dot V=\mu(aF+c\lambda),\quad \dot F=\mu(aV+c\lambda),\quad \dot\Omega_P=\alpha\lambda,\quad \dot\lambda=I_{\rm gate}.
\]
The neck of Theorem~\ref{nk:mig} lies on \(\Mk\) (both at \(\sigma=O(\kappa^{-2})\)).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The critical manifold \(\Mo=\{\sigma=0\}\) has transverse layer eigenvalue \(-r\le-r_*<0\): normally
hyperbolic attracting. Fenichel's theorem gives \(\eta=H(y,\epsilon)=O(\epsilon)\), i.e.\
\(\sigma=\epsilon\eta=O(\kappa^{-2})\); with \(\rho=O(\kappa^{-2})\to0\) the reduced field follows.
\end{proof}

On \(\Mk\), \(\dot\Delta=-(a\mu+\rho)\Delta=-a\mu\Delta+O(\kappa^{-2})\Delta\), so the diagonal

\begin{proof}

rate \(a\mu+\rho\ge a\mu_*+O(\kappa^{-2})\) is uniformly positive and below the \(\sigma\)-rate and
above the \(O(1)\) slow rates.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Concentration: ``many beginnings, one neck''}

\begin{proof}
By Theorems~\ref{nk:fen}--\ref{nk:diag} both reach \(\Mk\) then \(\Dk\) and, sharing slow data, the

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The hypothesis is \(\Af(t_c)>0\). Linearising \(\dot\qf=\Af\qf-\Bf\qf^3\) at \(\qf=0\) gives the
